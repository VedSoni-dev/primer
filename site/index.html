Someone is going to build the Primer.

Y Combinator asked for it by name. The technology is here. The honest half of the pitch is true — every child deserves the tutoring only wealthy children get today.

The other half is that whoever builds it ends up holding years of adaptation per child, millions of children, behind one login. That is not an education product. That is the most intimate influence infrastructure ever proposed.

So this repository exists to settle the terms while the record is still small enough to belong to families. One SQLite file. On your machine. Yours.

Read that as an investor and it means a large market. Read it as a parent and it means the reading app is the wedge, and the wedge goes somewhere. YC is not hiding this. They wrote it down. Believe them.

02 — the threat model

A primer is the book you read first.

To prime someone is to decide, before they decide anything, what will come easily to their mind later. That is the word. That has always been the word.

To teach a child well you need to know things about her that nobody else writes down. A good tutor holds all of it in their head for one year. A record holds it forever. That is exactly why it teaches — and exactly why it is dangerous.

Every line above is the kind of row that makes teaching work. Every line above is also a lever. The file does not know the difference. Only the terms do.

FAILURE MODE 01

The company dies and takes the child with it

Every education product ships an interface and treats the child's data as exhaust. Pivot, acquisition, or shutdown, and the record dies. The child starts over. Again. A first-grade teacher inherits a folder of worksheets and a reading level; everything a tutor actually needs evaporates in June.

FAILURE MODE 02

Portability becomes a feature they will never ship

Once one company holds a few years of a few million childhoods, export is a churn button. No growth team ships a churn button. Terms are settled early or not at all.

FAILURE MODE 03

The teaching goes into the weights

If the pedagogy lives inside a proprietary model, nobody can read it, fork it, or prove it wrong. A curriculum graph with a bad prerequisite edge quietly mis-teaches a real child, and no outsider can even file the bug.

FAILURE MODE 04

Engagement machinery aimed at a six-year-old

Streaks, coins, leaderboards, countdown timers. A twelve-minute session that ends on a win beats forty minutes that ends in tears — but only one of those two numbers shows up in a board deck.

03 — the terms, written as code

Open source here is not a license choice. It is the threat model.

Nothing on this page is a promise, a pledge, or a values page. Every item below is a constraint in the repository, and most of them have a test asserting them. That is the only kind of commitment that survives an acquisition.

01 The record is the product. The interface is disposable.

There is no app in the repository. On purpose. Anything that makes the record depend on a particular UI is a bug. The tutor generates a page for one child for one afternoon and throws it away; the file is the thing that lasts.

02 One file. On your machine. No sync, ever, in the core.

SQLite, 20 tables, in the family's home directory. Nothing phones home. There is no account, no server, and no API key to leave lying around a family's laptop.

03 Evidence over scores — auditable row by row.

Every number is derived from append-only observations kept forever. evidence_for returns the raw attempts behind any estimate, verbatim. When a parent asks “why does it think that,” there is an answer, not a vibe.

04 Export carries the whole history.

primer export / primer import round-trip everything — observations, sessions, generated activities, and the curriculum packs the history depends on. The format is documented and versioned in FORMAT.md so that this project is not the only thing able to read a child's record.

05 The child never talks to a model.

The fast loop — a child at play — never calls a model at all. She meets a static HTML page an adult approved. The model works on the record, not on the child.

06 A review gate no unattended model can lift.

Every generated activity lands in pending_review. No child reaches it until a human approves it. Rejections are read back on the next planning run, so saying no teaches the tutor instead of just blocking it.

07 The unattended run is caged, and the cage is tested.

No shell, no filesystem, no web, no inherited config, an explicit allowlist of record tools, and a hard budget ceiling enforced by the runtime rather than by our own accounting.

08 No engagement machinery. Structurally.

No streaks, coins, leaderboards, or countdown timers — and the record is not allowed to encode the opposite. There is nothing in the schema to optimize minutes-on-task with.

09 Deletable, and it means it.

primer delete --learner <name> --yes removes every row, plus that child's recordings and generated activities on disk, and reports any file it could not remove.

what the unattended tutor is allowed to touch
claude -p --tools ""                # every built-in tool off: no bash, no fs, no web
       --strict-mcp-config          # cannot see your other MCP servers
       --setting-sources ""         # no CLAUDE.md, no hooks, no plugins
       --mcp-config <primer only>   # reaches this record and nothing else
       --allowedTools mcp__primer__learner_context,…   # explicit allowlist
       --max-budget-usd <left today> # ceiling enforced outside our own accounting

The test suite asserts each part of this. Delete a flag, break a test.

What the autonomous loop can cannot do

  • create or delete learners
  • change accommodations
  • set goals

Those tools exist where a human is asking for them, and are deliberately absent from the unattended loop. If the tutor thinks an accommodation is wrong, it writes a note and a human decides.

04 — receipts

Nobody in this story has to be a villain.

That is the uncomfortable part. There is no cackling. There is a cap table, a growth target, and a renewal date, and those are enough. So this is not an accusation of anybody's character. It is a note about incentives, addressed to the people who will actually decide how this goes.

To YC

You wrote the honest sentence out loud: a parent buys it for basic skills, and it is “the entry point to far greater ambitions.” Good. Now fund the version where the ambition is not owning the child's file. Ask every founder who pitches this RFS one question: can the family walk out with the whole record, in a documented format, on day one? If the answer needs a slide, it is a no.

To the founder who wins this RFS

You will be told that portability is a v2 feature. It is not. It is the only feature that cannot be added later, because by v2 you will have a retention number that depends on it not existing. Build on an open record now, while it costs you nothing but a weekend, and you get to keep being the person you are today.

To the labs

The model is not the moat and you know it. The moat someone is about to build is the memory of a childhood. If long-horizon tutoring is coming, the state it runs on should be a file the family holds and any model can read — including your competitor's. Anything else and the education layer becomes lock-in with a children's-book skin.

To edtech

A decade of products shipped an interface and treated a child's learning history as exhaust. Then the company pivoted, or was acquired, or shut down, and the history went with it. That is not a rare tragedy, it is the default outcome, and every teacher reading this already knows it because they have re-assessed the same child three times in three years. Export is not a courtesy. It is the product.

To parents

The question to ask a tutoring app is not “is it adaptive.” It is: where does the file live, can I read it, can I take it, and who else has a copy? If the answer is a dashboard, the answer is no. You are not being paranoid. You are being the last gate, and this software is built to keep you in that job — the approval screen exists so a model never gets an unsupervised run at your kid.

To teachers and reading specialists

The curriculum graph in this repository is 213 skills, pre-K to grade 3, English only, and somewhere in it a prerequisite edge is wrong. Wrong edges quietly mis-teach real children. You are the only people who can find them. That is a contribution nobody at a startup can outspend you on.

To us — the honest sample size

One child. One week. The curriculum stops at grade 3. The BKT parameters are principled defaults, not fitted to data, and the fitting code refuses to run on a sample this small rather than print a confident number. Releases currently ship a Windows binary only. Anyone claiming more than that about v0.1 — including this page — should be checked against the repo.

05 — what is actually in the box

It is a record and a server. That is the whole trick.

Open Learner Record

20-table SQLite schema. Evidence append-only. Full spec in SPEC.md.

Mastery model

Bayesian Knowledge Tracing over a two-component forgetting curve. Recomputable from raw evidence at any time.

Scheduler

Ranks lapsed / due / stuck / frontier, spread across strands so a session is not eight vowel drills.

Placement

A cold start is placed, not assumed. Probes at the band age suggests, bisects to where the child actually is.

Curriculum

213 skills, pre-K–grade 3, reading / writing / math, with a prerequisite graph that crosses domains.

MCP server

28 tools. learner_context is the one that matters. Same implementations for the human and unattended paths.

Autonomous tutor

Drives claude -p on a loop against that server. Your existing login, no API key. Queue, review gate, budget.

Surface

Localhost. /k/<name> is the only URL a child needs; /review is the adult gate.

One runtime dependency: the MCP SDK. Storage is node:sqlite, built into Node 22+. The model is reached through the claude binary you already have.

06 — none of this matters if it stays small

The proprietary version will have consumer polish and a marketing budget.

That is a real fight and pretending otherwise is stupid. The open version wins on the three things a company structurally cannot offer, and all three are made of contributions: a corrected prerequisite, a curriculum pack past grade 3 or in another language, a bug report from a real child's week.

most needed

Curriculum beyond grade 3, and beyond English. A content job, not an engineering one.

most needed

Teachers and reading specialists auditing the prerequisite graph.

needed

needed

Real evidence samples so BKT parameters can be fitted instead of assumed.
